# Case context: what the eight food-analysis reports actually belong to

The four reference PDFs added to this repository change the reading of `Document 2.pdf`
completely. The eight Form VIIA certificates are **not routine market surveillance**. They
are laboratory evidence from a **mass food-poisoning incident at a residential school**,
with a criminal FIR registered the following day.

> Personal identifiers (Aadhaar/ID numbers, individual mobile numbers) that appear in the
> source PDFs are deliberately **not reproduced** here. Names of firms and of the office
> holders named in the FIR are retained because they are the subject of the documents.

## The incident

**Place:** अटल आवासीय विद्यालय धौर्रा — **Atal Awasiya Vidyalaya, Dhaurra**, village Dhaurra,
PS Jakhlaun, District Lalitpur, Uttar Pradesh (recorded as 16 km south-east of the police
station).

These are Uttar Pradesh residential schools for the children of registered construction
workers, administered under the Labour Department — which is why the contractor's letter
is addressed to the **Deputy Labour Commissioner (उपश्रमायुक्त), Jhansi Mandal**.

**What happened:** On **16 July 2026**, out of roughly **700 students present**, about
**49–50 children** developed **abdominal pain and vomiting** and were admitted to
**District Hospital, Lalitpur**.

The two accounts of scale agree closely:

| FIR (Principal's complaint) | कुल ४९ बच्चों — 49 children, stomach pain and vomiting |
| Contractor's letter | ~700 present, ~50 children, mainly stomach pain; "children are now healthy" |

### Sections invoked — Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

| Section | Offence |
|---|---|
| **125** | Act endangering life or personal safety of others |
| **271** | Negligent act likely to spread infection of disease dangerous to life |
| **274** | **Adulteration of food or drink intended for sale** |
| **275** | **Sale of noxious food or drink**, knowing or having reason to believe it unfit for consumption |

This matters for reading the lab reports: BNS 274 and 275 are the food-adulteration
offences, and 275 in particular requires the food to be **noxious / unfit for
consumption** — a materially higher bar than "substandard" under section 3(1)(zx) of the
FSS Act 2006. See [`reused_oil_review.md`](reused_oil_review.md) for why the laboratory
evidence does not reach that bar.

### The complaint's theory of the case

The Principal's *tehrir* attributes the illness to **negligence and gross indifference
(लापरवाही व घोर उदासीनता)** on two fronts:

1. **Food preparation and the upkeep of supplied materials** — vegetables and other
   ingredients — by the catering contractor (Shalu Associates), which held the mess
   contract through a **tender process**.
2. **Cleanliness (साफ-सफाई सही से न होने के कारण)** of the school and the mess, laid at the
   door of the housekeeping contractor (Nirmal Facility Management Services).

## The contractor's response

Letter from **Shalu Associates** (signed Braj Kishore, Proprietor) to the Deputy Labour
Commissioner, Jhansi Mandal. Its substance:

- The firm has provided the mess at the school **since October 2025** (~9 months), it says
  without any prior food-related complaint.
- The school reopened after summer vacation on **01 July 2026**; children returned from
  home, **some carrying snacks and food from home**, which they **shared with other
  students**.
- It requests a **complete ban on outside food** in the school premises, arguing such
  sharing raises infection risk and exposes the mess to unfair blame.
- It confirms ~700 students present on 16/07/2026 and ~50 affected, and that medical care
  was arranged with the district administration; children have recovered.
- Remedial action taken: **a large RO water unit has been installed**.
- It requests **CCTV in the kitchen and all dining areas**.

Two things are worth noting about this letter. First, it is a **defence document**: its
central factual claim — outside food brought from home — shifts causation away from the
mess. Second, the remedial measure it volunteers is a **water purifier**, which implies
the contractor's own working hypothesis was **waterborne** contamination. No water sample
appears anywhere in the eight laboratory reports.

**Note on report TC-1377926000007239F (cooked rice):** the report states the sample was

consistent on these dates. Likely a clerical slip, but on an exhibit in a criminal matter
it is the kind of defect that invites challenge.

## Which samples were enforcement, and which were survey

The sample codes fall into two series, and the split is significant:

| Series | Codes | Samples |
|---|---|---|
| **`FSDA/UP/SURV/…`** (survey) | 86426–86430 | cooked rice, kadhi, roti, **dahi (86428)**, **oil from the kadhai (86430)** |
| **`FSDA/UP/…`** (enforcement) | 940230–940232 | besan (Tulsi Gold), dahi (Samooh brand), **sealed refined soyabean oil** |

The three formal enforcement samples are all **packaged, branded, standardised
commodities** — precisely the articles that *can* be prosecuted against a compositional
standard. The **cooked food actually served to the children**, and the **oil actually in
the frying vessel**, were taken as survey samples.

That is legally coherent but investigatively awkward: it means the items closest to the
alleged cause of illness carry the weakest evidentiary status. This is developed in
[`reused_oil_review.md`](reused_oil_review.md).

## The gap that dominates everything

**Not one of the eight reports contains any microbiological testing.** Across all eight
certificates the parameters are: proximate composition, oil chemistry, adulterant
screens, microscopy, six heavy metals and up to twenty pesticide residues.

There is **no** total plate count, no coliforms, no *E. coli*, *Salmonella*,
*Staphylococcus aureus*, *Bacillus cereus*, no yeast/mould, and no water analysis.

For an acute outbreak in which ~49 children vomited within a day, that is the decisive
omission. Vomiting-predominant illness with rapid onset points to preformed bacterial
toxin (*S. aureus*, *B. cereus*) or to waterborne infection — none of which any of these
tests can detect. The FIR expressly invokes **BNS 271, spreading infection**, yet the
laboratory work contains nothing capable of establishing infection.

The chemistry in `Document 2.pdf` can establish that some food supplied to the school was
**of substandard quality**. It cannot establish **what made the children ill**.
