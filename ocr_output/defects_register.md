# Defects register — all eight Form VIIA reports

Every entry below was checked against the 180 dpi scan, not inferred from OCR alone.
Where a finding could not be confirmed it is marked **VERIFY** rather than asserted.

Severity: **A** = goes to admissibility or to the verdict itself · **B** = materially weakens
the report · **C** = clerical, but it is an exhibit in a criminal matter.

---

## Systemic — affects the whole set

### S1. No microbiological testing anywhere · **A**
Across all eight certificates there is no total plate count, no coliforms, no *E. coli*,
*Salmonella*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Bacillus cereus*, no yeast or mould. The parameters
tested are composition, oil chemistry, adulterant screens, microscopy, six heavy metals and
up to twenty pesticide residues.

The FIR pleads **BNS 271 — negligent act likely to spread infection of disease dangerous to
life.** Nothing in this laboratory work is capable of establishing infection. In a
vomiting-predominant outbreak of ~49 children with same-day onset, the probable agents are
preformed bacterial toxins or a waterborne organism, and none of them is detectable by any
test performed.

### S2. No water sample · **A**
The catering contractor's own remedial measure was to install an RO unit, which means the
contractor's working hypothesis was the water supply. No water was analysed.

### S3. The most probative samples were lifted as *survey* samples · **A**
| Series | Codes | Samples |
|---|---|---|
| `FSDA/UP/SURV/…` | 86426–86430 | cooked rice, kadhi, roti, dahi, **oil from the kadhai** |
| `FSDA/UP/…` (enforcement) | 940230–940232 | besan, dahi (Samooh), **sealed refined soyabean oil** |

The three formal enforcement samples are all packaged, branded, standardised commodities.
The **food actually served to the children** and the **oil actually in the frying vessel**
carry the weaker survey status. The sampling notices appear to record the purpose in
handwriting as **सर्वेक्षण जाँच** (survey analysis), consistent with the `SURV` prefix,
though the handwriting is not fully legible. **VERIFY** against the Form VI memoranda.

### S4. "BLOQ" is reported without any limit of quantification · **A**
Every heavy metal and every pesticide result in the set is reported as **BLOQ**. The only
explanation given anywhere is a footnote on page 10 expanding the acronym. **No numerical
LOQ is stated for any analyte on any report.**

Without the LOQ, BLOQ is not an interpretable result. Lead in dahi carries an MRL of
**0.02 mg/L**; if the method's LOQ were, say, 0.05 mg/L, then "BLOQ" is consistent with a
lead concentration **above** the MRL. The reports as drafted cannot distinguish compliance
from non-detection, for any of the roughly 60 analyte results in the batch.

### S5. All samples destroyed after certification · **B**
Recorded on every report. Nothing can be re-analysed on the laboratory's portion. Whether
counterpart parts survive with the Designated Officer is not shown in this document set —
this bears on the FBO's re-analysis right under s.46(4) FSS Act. **VERIFY.**

### S6. No statement of measurement uncertainty · **B**
No report states measurement uncertainty or method reproducibility. This matters most where
a verdict rests on a narrow excursion — see D8.

### S7. Accreditation scope is declared inconsistently · **B**
The two dahi reports and the besan report divide results into **Part-A (covered under NABL)**
and **Part-B (not covered under NABL)**. The two oil reports and the three prepared-food
reports contain **no accreditation declaration at all**, so the accredited status of those
results is simply unknown from the face of the document.

### S8. Only three of eight sampling notices are in the record · **C**
Rule 2.4.1(3) notices are present for three samples only. The remaining five, and all Form VI
memoranda, are absent from this document set.

## Report TC-1377926000007243F — kadhai oil (page 2)

The weakest report in the set. Reviewed in full in [`reused_oil_review.md`](reused_oil_review.md).

### D1. No standard applied, stated twelve times over · **A**
Regulation field reads `***`. The "Prescribed Standards" column is **not blank** — every one
of the twelve rows contains an explicit **dash**. The laboratory affirmatively recorded that
no standard applies, then measured twelve parameters against nothing.

### D2. The "Substandard" verdict has no author · **A**
The analyst gave no adverse opinion. "Substandard" for this sample appears only in the
Designated Officer's Hindi summary at page 12. Section 3(1)(zx) requires failure against
*specified* standards; the certificate specifies none.

### D3. No opinion · **A**
In full: *"On the basis of tests performed, analytical data are as above."* A data
transmittal, not an opinion. The other four substantive reports in the batch give reasoned
opinions with statutory citations.

### D4. Two results cited but not reported · **A**
| Row | Test | Method cited | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Test for mineral oil | FSSAI 02.029.2021 | **–** |
| 10 | Test for Vitamin A | FSSAI 02.040.2021 | **–** |

Confirmed by ink density (75–80 dark pixels vs 600–1,900 for populated cells) and visually.
**The sealed-oil sample, same analyst, same week, reports both** — mineral oil *Negative*,
Vitamin A *Positive*. Mineral oil is an adulteration screen; its presence would move the
article from substandard toward unsafe, which is where BNS 274/275 operate.

### D5. Total Polar Compounds never measured · **A**
TPC ≤ 25% is the only enforceable limit that applies to used frying oil. It was not run. The
sample is now destroyed, so it cannot be obtained.

### D6. Peroxide value presented without the caveat that makes it meaningless · **B**
5.18 Meq/kg against a limit of 10 reads as a pass. Hydroperoxides decompose above frying
temperature, so used frying oil characteristically returns a *low* peroxide value. Valid for
stored oil; not for oil out of a hot kadhai. No caveat is given.

## Report TC-1377926000007237F — besan, Tulsi Gold (pages 10–11)

### D7. The entire adverse verdict rests on a non-accredited parameter · **A**
All **Part-A** parameters — moisture, ash insoluble in dilute HCl, alcoholic acidity, khesari
dal (BOAA), synthetic colour — **passed**.

The substandard finding rests solely on **microscopy** (FSSAI Manual 03:024:2023), which the
report itself places under *"Part-B (Parameters not covered in NABL Scope)"*: **"Gram
starches are seen along with wheat & pea starches."**

So the one finding that condemns the sample is the one the laboratory declares to be outside
its accredited scope. It is also a **subjective, operator-dependent** determination with no
retained micrograph in the record, on a sample since destroyed.

### D8. Regulation cited as "2.4.4" — needs checking · **VERIFY / B**
The report reads *"I found the sample to be Besan falling under regulate on no **2.4.4** of
food safety and standards (Food products and Food Additive) Regulation 2011."*

The form's own heading cites *"rule 2.4.2(5) & **2.4.4**(6)"* — the procedural rule for the
Food Analyst's report. The same number appearing as the product standard may be coincidence
(the FSS Product Standards Regulations do use a 2.4.x series for cereals and cereal products)
or may be a transcription of the rule number into the regulation field. **I could not confirm
the correct clause for besan and am not asserting this is wrong** — it should be checked
against the current Chapter 2.4 of the FSS (Food Products Standards and Food Additives)
Regulations 2011.

### D9. Referenced attachment absent · **C**
The report carries a "Reasons:" field referring to material *"attached with form-6"*. No
Form VI is in this document set.

### D10. Mercury MRL cell unresolved · **VERIFY / C**
In the page-11 heavy-metals table, MRL values are given for lead (2.5), copper (30), arsenic
(1.1), tin (250) and cadmium (1.5); the mercury MRL did not resolve under OCR. Check whether
the cell is populated on the original.

## Reports TC-1377926000007241F and TC-1377926000007238F — dahi (pages 6–9)

### D11. Milk class not established on the record · **B**
Both were judged against the **4.5% mixed-milk** minimum. The standards column also lists
3.0% (toned), 1.3% (double toned) and ≤0.5% (skimmed). No label declaration or milk class
is recorded anywhere in either report to justify choosing mixed milk.

The verdict survives regardless — at 0.64% and 0.53% the samples fail every class, including
skimmed, whose maximum is 0.5% — but the basis for the comparator is undocumented.

### D12. The confirmatory fat test could not be performed · **B**
"B.R. of extracted fat at 40 °C" is reported as **Insufficient** on both. There was too
little fat to run it, so the milk-fat figure stands without its normal cross-check.

### D13. Heavy metals and pesticides are outside accredited scope · **B**
All Part-B, and all reported as BLOQ without an LOQ (see S4).

## Report TC-1377926000007240F — prepared kadhi (page 4)

### D14. Positive adulterant result contradicted by the opinion · **A**
Row 5: **"Boric Acid Test for Turmeric — Positive"** (FSSAI 10.036:2021), with the
prescribed-standard cell left blank. The Opinion nonetheless reads *"no adulterant is
detected,"* and the covering summary records **Pass**.

Boric acid is not a permitted food additive. The row may be a naming artefact — turmeric
paper is the classical reagent *for* detecting boric acid, so the row may be recording
detection of turmeric rather than of boric acid — but **as printed, the result and the
opinion contradict each other**, on a dish made from besan, in a batch where the besan was
found adulterated. This is the single most important unresolved item after the oil report.

## Report TC-1377926000007236F — sealed refined soyabean oil (page 1)

### D17. A sealed sample fails a compositional index, and nobody asks why · **A**
Saponification value **196.8** against a range of 189–195, and acid value **0.91** against a
maximum of 0.6 — in a tin recorded as **"Intact and Unbroken."**

Saponification value is a compositional property reflecting mean fatty-acid chain length.
**Degradation cannot explain an out-of-range saponification value in oil that was never
heated.** In unopened stock it points instead to the oil not being what it is sold as — poor
refining, or blending with a cheaper oil of different chain length.

The report records the failure, attributes it to "Saponification value and Acid value exceed
the standard limit," and stops. No authenticity question is raised and, on this record, the
oil was not traced back to its supplier. For a school feeding ~700 children, the supply of
misdescribed oil is a larger issue than the single tin.

### D18. The saponification excursion is narrow and no uncertainty is stated · **B**
196.8 against a ceiling of 195 is a 0.9% excursion. With no stated measurement uncertainty
(S6), that finding is contestable on reproducibility alone. The acid value excursion (52%
over) is not.

## What this adds up to

Three of the five adverse verdicts are solid on their face: the two dahi reports fail on an
**accredited Part-A parameter** by a margin far beyond any analytical dispute, roughly
one-eighth of the legal minimum.

The other two are fragile in different ways. The **besan** verdict rests entirely on a
**non-accredited, subjective microscopy call** with no retained image and no surviving sample
(D7). The **kadhai oil** verdict rests on **nothing the analyst was willing to sign** (D1–D5).

And the set as a whole cannot answer the question the FIR actually asks. It establishes that
some food supplied to the school was of substandard quality. On what made 49 children vomit,
it is silent — and, because of S1, S2 and S5, permanently so.
